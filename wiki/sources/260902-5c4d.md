# Local CPU and memory are the wrong meters for agent-worker concurrency — the machine is not what a worker consumes

Measured before choosing an instrument, because the instrument was the whole
question.

**CPU.** Five concurrent subagents, sampled at 1 Hz for 89 s: mean 14.3% of
1000% available, peak 160%. About 0.32 core each at peak. 80% of a 10-core
machine is 800%, so a CPU-derived cap permits ~25 workers at peak cost and
~270 at mean cost.

**Memory.** Session RSS 2.28 -> 2.80 GiB over the same burst, ~104 MiB per
concurrent worker. 80% of 32 GiB permits ~180.

**Processes.** Top-level `claude` process count did not move: 6 -> 7. A
subagent is a request stream inside a session process, not an OS process, so
no per-process limit is approached.

**Latency.** Identical trivial prompt (16,360 prompt tokens, no tools):
concurrency 1 = 1579 ms; concurrency 12 = mean 1646 ms, median 1627, stdev
104, spread 1482-1874. +4% for 12x the work. Serial queuing would have put
the twelfth at 19.8 s; it landed at 1.9 s. No knee at 12.

So no local meter binds at a number a person would choose. What a worker
actually consumes is the model service — rate limit and spend — which the
machine cannot read. A cap derived from "80% of the machine" is effectively
unbounded.

**What load IS good for:** throttling down. `os.getloadavg()[0]` on a 10-core
box, budget (0.8*cores - load1)/0.32: 12 slots while load < 4.2, then 10, 9,
6, 3, and the floor at load >= 7.5. Verified live at both ends — idle gave 12,
a 12-core burn gave 1.

**Caveat measured, not assumed:** load1 is a 1-minute average and lags badly.
Six busy cores moved it only 2.14 -> 3.31 in 20 s (throttle never engaged);
after a 12-core burn ended it read 20.58 and stayed high for minutes. A
dispatcher metering on load1 stays throttled well after the machine is free.

**macOS gotcha:** vm_stat "Pages free" is not available memory. Counting only
free+speculative reported 31.2 of 32 GiB used on an idle machine and drove the
meter to its floor. Add inactive and purgeable.
