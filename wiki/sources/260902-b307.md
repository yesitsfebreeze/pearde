# load1 over-reports for minutes after a burn; a one-second instantaneous sample corrects it

`os.getloadavg()[0]` is a one-minute average and lags in both directions.
Measured: six busy cores moved it 2.14 → 3.31 in 20 s (it under-reports a
machine filling up); after a 12-core burn ended it read 20.58 and stayed high
for minutes (it over-reports a machine that is already free). A concurrency
meter reading load1 alone therefore throttles late and recovers late.

An instantaneous sample corrects the recovery half. On darwin
`top -l 2 -n 0 -s 1`'s second sample gives `CPU usage: u% user, s% sys, i% idle`
over that one-second window; on other posix two reads of `/proc/stat` a second
apart give the same. Cost measured at 1.5-1.9 s of wall clock, so it is worth
asking only on the throttle path — never on a quiet machine, whose count is
already at the ceiling.

Proven live in one run: load1 read 11.78 on a 10-core machine, which alone
drives a 0.80-target meter to its floor of 1, while the instantaneous sample
read 47% busy; the corrected count was 10. In a separate run on a genuinely
saturated machine load1 18.14 and idle 0.0% agreed, and the count correctly
stayed at the floor.

Residual weakness: the confirming sample is a one-second window, so a machine
that is bursty rather than busy can read either way. A floor of 1 is what makes
that safe — the worst reading still makes progress.
