# Using the Microsoft or Teams logo on a third-party site needs a formal licence; naming Teams in plain text does not

Microsoft's "logo third party usage guidance" (Aug 2025) and the general
trademark usage page are explicit: "our logos, app and product icons,
illustrations, photographs, videos, and designs can never be used without an
express license." The guidance names only two unlicensed exceptions — a page
that exclusively sells Microsoft products, or marketing collateral in which the
Microsoft logo appears alongside "the corporate logo of at least one other
company in which you have a similar relationship."

An integrations page listing many vendors does not fit either. So a Teams icon
on a marketing site is out until a partner agreement exists.

The NAME is a different matter and is explicitly allowed. The guidance's own
worked example is "Contoso app is certified for Microsoft Teams" — truthful,
referential use of the word, provided it does not imply affiliation,
endorsement, sponsorship or certification that is not held, and does not read
more prominently than your own brand.

Microsoft says nothing at all about a stylised, drawn recreation of the Teams
interface; screenshots are routed to a separate copyright-permissions page.
Silence plus the endorsement clause is the whole of the guidance here.
