# a background job ignores SIGINT and its children inherit it

A non-interactive shell starting a background job (`( … ) &`) sets SIGINT and
SIGQUIT to SIG_IGN for it, and every child inherits that disposition — POSIX
XCU 2.11, asynchronous lists.

A probe that replays a user's Ctrl+C with `killpg` therefore measures the
runner's job control instead of the interrupt whenever a harness sweep runs
it. Measured 2026-09-03 on this board: the `a-worker-survives-the-window-that-
launched-it` probe was 48/48 standalone and 46/48 under four-way parallel
`( … ) &`, the same shape `doctor.sh --harnesses` uses — the two reds were the
SIGINT section, every time, not a race.

The fix is to restore the disposition explicitly, in the fixture
(`signal.signal(signal.SIGINT, signal.default_int_handler)`) and in every
child (`subprocess.Popen(…, preexec_fn=lambda: signal.signal(signal.SIGINT,
signal.SIG_DFL))`). After it: 48/48 four ways in parallel.
